Research

Two sessions on using agentic AI in empirical research.

  • Session 1 · PDF

    An introduction to generative AI

    What an LLM actually is, why it sounds confident when it is wrong, and where the field stands. Chat versus agentic tools, Claude Code versus Codex, pricing and model tiers. How the agent works, what a context window is, and the four checks for errors that do not crash. Teaching when students have the same tools, and what all of this means for academic work.

  • Session 2 · PDF

    Practical workflows

    A referee report on your own draft with the /review-paper skill, and a subagent that critiques the report. Slides and explorable explanations built from a paper with /explorable-deck. A voice.md from your own writing, so that AI edits sound like you. Checking that the code does what the paper says with /review-paper-code, and why git diffs help agents. A quiz bank from your lecture notes. Working with an agent when the data sits on a secure server it cannot reach.

    Workshop syllabus

    The one-page outline of both sessions and the optional block.

Teaching

Practical AI for Teaching, a one-day workshop for university teachers. In the morning an agent writes a thesis during the lecture, and in the afternoon participants assess that thesis and build tools for their own courses.

  • Session 1 · Morning · PDF

    An introduction to generative AI

    Six topics. Fundamentals and tools: chat versus agents, how Copilot Chat and Claude Code differ, and how the agent works. Supervision and assessment of theses, with a thesis an agent writes during the session and AI detectors and their limits. Teaching in general, and why take-home grades stopped measuring learning. Skills. Replication packages and verification. Hallucination, copyright, and plagiarism.

  • Session 2 · Afternoon · PDF

    Practical workflows

    Hands-on, on the thesis the agent wrote in the morning. Feedback against a rubric and a second opinion on that feedback, defence questions, a skill built from your own rubric, an AI policy for a course, a quiz bank from your lecture notes, a course update, and one task of your own.

Files for the Session 2 exercises: paper.pdf, the thesis the agent writes in Session 1, and rubric.md, an example rubric you can replace with your own.
