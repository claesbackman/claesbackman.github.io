Just-in-time financial knowledge from AI assistants

Advice, learning and spillovers

Claes Bäckman · Philipp Schirmer

Leibniz Institute for Financial Research SAFE

Three students, 100 euros each, one question: what should I buy?

Set the scene in two sentences: three students in the same lab, each with a Trade Republic account and 100 euros from us, the same menu of stocks, ETFs and bonds. Mia gets fact pages, Lena an AI tutor, Jonas an AI advisor.

Then the bet, hands up: who holds the better portfolio when they leave tonight? And who knows more about investing in April? Let the room commit. The answer slide is 14; everything in between is why the question is worth 300 students and 47,600 euros.

AI assistants make financial advice essentially free, and students already use them

Two-thirds of the Bonn students we surveyed use an AI chatbot every week. More than half had asked one a financial question in the past three months.

A third hold a Trade Republic account. They can make a real investment in the lab on their own account.

Pre-pilot survey, University of Bonn, September 2026, n = 104. Recruitment detail in the appendix.

Three measured numbers, nothing stylised here. The first two say the technology is already in the room. The third is the feasibility number for the design: a real purchase needs a real account. Taxes, budgeting, insurance and investing were the most common financial topics asked about.

Four questions that we want to answer

  • Does access to an AI assistant at the moment of decision improve the choice?
  • Does the knowledge persist, or does it decay like classroom education?
  • Does it spill over to decisions made later without the assistant, including participants’ own portfolios?
  • (Does the effect work through advice or through learning?)

Read the four as the structure of the rest of the talk: real money answers the first, the six-month follow-up answers the second and third, the two treatment arms answer the fourth. The literature line is the whole literature slide for a 20-minute talk; expand in questions if asked.

Design: real money, three arms, six months

Three hundred students invest 100 euros of real money on their own account

300 students with brokerage accounts, Frankfurt Laboratory for Experimental Economic Research

100 € invested for real on the participant’s own Trade Republic account, paid by us against the order confirmation

6 months until the follow-up survey and the second portfolio upload

The lab controls the information environment. At arrival, everyone uploads a screenshot of their brokerage account, so we see the portfolios.

Why the lab and not online: the control arm must be AI-free, and in 2027 that is only enforceable in a room. Why own accounts: the holding decision six months later is real, with real taxes and real regret. Follow-up rounds possible at BonnEconLab, Mainz or Darmstadt.

If asked how the money reaches them: two routes, both in the appendix. The default is reimbursement against the verified order.

Three arms

Control Fact pages + web search

AI tutor AI model that teaches the concept

AI Advisor AI model that gives short advice about specific assets.

Primary comparison: access, Lena and Jonas against Mia. Secondary comparison: mechanism, Lena against Jonas.

Six months later we look at the position, the whole portfolio and the trust

Did they keep the study position or sell it?

Did the rest of the portfolio change? Compare the second screenshot of their own account with the first to test spillovers.

Do they use and trust AI for money now? And how do they feel about the advice, once they know how it turned out.

The follow-up is what separates a durable change from a one-time nudge. The portfolio re-upload is the expensive part of the design and the reason the follow-up fee is paid. The last point is a free experiment inside the experiment: two participants with the same advice and different market luck.

Our open questions

  • Randomize across subjects or within subject?
  • How many decisions and what kind? How to incentivize each?
  • How to ensure that we get a large sample in the follow up?
  • How to best observe people’s behavior in the non-AI arm? Which process data to collect?
  • Two arms or three? What is the legal requirement on giving legal advice?

Does AI make the decision better, or the decision-maker better?

Appendix

Two ways to put 100 real euros into a participant’s account

Reimbursement

Participants execute the orders on their own Trade Republic account in the laboratory.

We pay the 100 euros plus the show-up fee against the verified order confirmations.

Direct purchase

We purchase the assets the participant chose and transfer them to the participant’s Trade Republic account.

Both routes keep the asset in the participant’s own account, so the holding decision at six months is theirs and real. Back to the design.

One in six Bonn students can be recruited for a real-money lab session

32% hold a Trade Republic account (32 of 101 students)

53% of those would come to a BonnEconLab session (17 of 32)

17% joint: a Trade Republic account and willing to come (17 of 101)

About half of respondents hold a securities account of any kind. Two-thirds use an AI chatbot at least weekly and 58 percent asked one a financial question in the past three months.

Pre-pilot survey, University of Bonn, 14 September 2026, n = 104. A realistic Bonn lab sample is 100 to 200. Back to the pilot numbers.

What 300 participants can detect

Session effects of a third to a half of a standard deviation are detectable. The follow-up contrasts are powered for moderate effects once the baseline is conditioned on. The tutor-versus-advisor comparison on six-month outcomes is exploratory. Back to the design.
