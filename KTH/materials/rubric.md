# Assessment rubric: degree project, first cycle

Each criterion is assessed on four levels: **excellent**, **good**, **sufficient** and **insufficient**. A thesis must reach at least *sufficient* on every criterion to pass.

## 1. Research question and motivation

- **Excellent:** The question is precise and answerable with the data. The motivation explains why the answer matters and for whom. The introduction states what the thesis adds.
- **Good:** The question is clear and the motivation relevant, but the contribution is stated in general terms.
- **Sufficient:** The question can be identified, but it is broad, or the thesis answers a somewhat different question.
- **Insufficient:** No clear question, or the analysis does not address it.

## 2. Literature and hypotheses

- **Excellent:** The review is organised around the question, compares studies, and identifies a gap the thesis fills. Hypotheses follow from the review and are all tested.
- **Good:** Relevant studies are covered and connected to the question. Hypotheses are stated and tested.
- **Sufficient:** Relevant studies are summarised one by one with little comparison. Hypotheses are stated.
- **Insufficient:** Key literature is missing or misrepresented, or sources cannot be verified.

## 3. Data and method

- **Excellent:** Data sources, sample and variable construction are fully documented. The method fits the question, its assumptions are stated, and threats to the interpretation (omitted variables, selection, measurement) are discussed and addressed.
- **Good:** Data and method are documented and appropriate. Main threats are mentioned.
- **Sufficient:** Data and method are described, but documentation has gaps or the main threats are not discussed.
- **Insufficient:** The sample or method cannot be reconstructed, or the method cannot answer the question.

## 4. Results and analysis

- **Excellent:** Results are reported accurately, with magnitudes interpreted in economic terms. The text matches the tables and figures. Claims are calibrated to what the method can show.
- **Good:** Results are accurate and interpreted, with minor gaps.
- **Sufficient:** Results are reported, but interpretation is thin or some claims go beyond the evidence.
- **Insufficient:** Results are misreported, or the text contradicts the tables.

## 5. Discussion, conclusions and sustainability

- **Excellent:** Conclusions follow from the results. Limitations are specific and their consequences discussed. Policy or sustainability implications are stated with appropriate caution.
- **Good:** Conclusions follow from the results, and limitations are discussed.
- **Sufficient:** Conclusions are stated, but limitations are generic or implications overstated.
- **Insufficient:** Conclusions do not follow from the results.

## 6. Structure, language and references

- **Excellent:** Clear structure, precise language, consistent numbers throughout. Every reference is complete and verifiable, and every cited source is used accurately.
- **Good:** Clear structure and language, with minor inconsistencies.
- **Sufficient:** Readable, but with inconsistencies in numbers, missing figures or incomplete references.
- **Insufficient:** Hard to follow, or references are missing or unverifiable.
