Overview

Housing markets are shaped by a wide set of frictions that impede price discovery, distort allocations, and amplify distributional consequences. These can reflect financial constraints, the search and matching process, market liquidity and transaction costs, or can result from individual behavioral preferences, imperfect information, and institutional characteristics.

The goal of the Workshop in Real Estate Finance is to discuss and support the development of new theoretical and empirical frameworks that quantify these frictions, identify transmission mechanisms, and evaluate how they interact across demand and supply. The program includes work on households, lenders, developers, landlords, intermediaries, and on how macroprudential regulation propagates through markets to shape prices, quantities, mobility, and access to homeownership.

Each presentation is allocated 25 minutes, followed by 15 minutes for the discussion and 5 minutes for general Q&A.
