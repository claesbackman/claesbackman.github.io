One Advisor for the Whole World?

Cross-Country Evidence on Financial Advice from AI

Claes Bäckman
Leibniz Institute for Financial Research SAFE

Abstract

Households around the world increasingly take portfolio advice from the same handful of AI models. How does that advice vary across countries, and does the advice track local fundamentals? I pose different portfolio problems to leading models across twenty-one countries in different languages, varying whether the questions invoke local context. When asked about the risky share, the advice is nearly uniform across countries but varies widely across models. Explanations for the allocation advice consistently invoke local context, but this has almost no impact on the risky share. When asked whether to prepay a mortgage or how to protect savings from inflation, the advice differs sharply across countries. The models follow retail financial advice, and only selectively incorporate the household’s balance sheet even when it is stated. While automated advice once held out the promise of tailoring guidance to everyone, AI models encode the same rule for everyone.

JEL: G11, G41, G51, D14, O33 · Keywords: financial advice, household finance, portfolio choice, robo-advising, artificial intelligence, large language models

How to read this site

  • The paper is split into the pages listed on the left, in the paper's own section numbering. Figures are drawn from the estimates and can be switched to a table or downloaded. Tables are the paper's tables.
  • Footnotes open inline when you click the small numbered buttons. Citations link to the reference list. Equation, figure and table numbers match the PDF.
  • The country effects explorer shows every country contrast for the three questions, the same contrast model by model, the leave-one-model-out estimates, and the cell means behind the language, local-context, age and stated-investor comparisons.
  • The prompt explorer shows the exact text every model received, in every country, version and question, with the DeepL back-translation for the non-English prompts.
  • The model tracking page sets the July 2026 panel beside the September 2026 wave of twenty-one models, fourteen of them new, on the same English slice of the primary question.
  • The raw model responses are not public. The data and code page says what the site does and does not contain, and gives a prompt for asking an AI assistant about the paper.
